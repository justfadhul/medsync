# Consults, review requests and duty rotas: web findings

Researched 24 Nov 2026. Each finding has a tag showing how well it was checked:
- **[V]** I read the primary page or PDF myself.
- **[S]** Taken from a search-result summary or a secondary source. The URL is real, but I did not read the full text.
- **[U]** Could not verify. Treat as a lead, not a fact.

## 1. FHIR and IHE: modelling consults, referrals and availability

1. **[V] ServiceRequest covers referrals and consults.** FHIR R4 says ServiceRequest can carry what a referral or transfer of care needs, including a specialist consultation or a second opinion. https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/servicerequest.html
2. **[V] ServiceRequest codes.** status: draft, active, on-hold, revoked, completed, entered-in-error, unknown. intent: proposal, plan, directive, order, original-order, reflex-order, filler-order, instance-order, option. priority: routine, urgent, asap, stat. All three value sets are *required* bindings. https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/servicerequest.html
3. **[V] Who is asked, and why.** `performer` (0..*) can point to Practitioner, PractitionerRole, Organization, CareTeam, HealthcareService and others. `performerType` (0..1) holds the *kind* of performer wanted (for example "cardiology registrar") when no person is named. `reasonReference` can point to Condition, Observation, DiagnosticReport or DocumentReference. `category` is only an example binding. https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/servicerequest.html
4. **[V] A request does not track its own fulfilment.** The Request pattern says so directly: ServiceRequest.status reflects only the authorisation, and fulfilment is tracked by Task. One request can have several Tasks. https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/request.html
5. **[V] Request and Event pattern.** Requests are proposals, plans or orders. Events are the activity once it is done. An event points back to its request (`basedOn`), and pairings are loose: a ServiceRequest may be answered by an Encounter, Procedure or DiagnosticReport. So a consult note (DocumentReference, Composition or Encounter) would point back to the consult ServiceRequest. https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/workflow.html
6. **[V] Task status codes (R4, same 12 in R5).** draft, requested, received (a possible performer has claimed it and is deciding), accepted (agreed, not started), rejected (declined before acting), ready, cancelled, in-progress, on-hold, failed, completed, entered-in-error. https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/valueset-task-status.html
7. **[V] Task fields that matter for consults.** `focus` is the request being fulfilled. `basedOn` is the higher-level authority behind it. `owner` is who is responsible *now*. `requester` is who asked. `businessStatus` holds local sub-states (for example "seen, note pending"). `restriction.period` is the window in which fulfilment is wanted, and it applies only when `focus` is a request. https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/task.html
8. **[V] R5 Task adds** `requestedPerformer` (CodeableReference: roles or people), `requestedPeriod`, `executionPeriod`, `statusReason` (why it was held, failed or refused) and a `performer` backbone with `function` and `actor`. https://hl7.org/fhir/R5/task.html
9. **[V] The FHIR exchange patterns.** FHIR lists options A to K. A to E are ad-hoc exchanges without Task. F to K use Task: F (Task on the placer's system), G (Task posted to the fulfiller), H (Task posted to a workflow broker), I (Task plus a sub-Task on the placer's side), J (messaging), K (custom operation). FHIR itself says the Task resource holds the workflow state. https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/workflow-communications.html
10. **[V] Option H, the broker.** A broker finds unassigned requests, posts Tasks to fulfillers and reissues them when one is rejected. It needs a broker that knows every filler, what each can do, and the cancellation rules. The section is marked "needs review". Option F suits single-system cases where state tracking matters and the request is not tied to one filler, such as "open" tasks that anyone on a team can claim. https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/workflow-management.html
11. **[V] Encounter roles.** Encounter.participant.type includes ADM (admitter), ATND (attender, oversees care during the encounter), CON (consultant, advises and recommends), REF (referrer) and DIS (discharger). The consultant can be attached to the encounter without becoming the attender. https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/valueset-encounter-participant-type.html
12. **[V] CareTeam can model "the team responsible for this patient".** It has status, category, subject, encounter and period, plus participants, each with `role`, `member`, `onBehalfOf` and their own `period`. A CareTeam can also exist with no patient (a code-blue team, say). ServiceRequest.performer and Task.owner may reference a CareTeam. https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/careteam.html
13. **[S] BSeR (Bidirectional Services eReferral) IG.** The Referral Task holds the state (created, accepted, in progress, completed), with initiator = Task.requester, recipient = Task.owner, focus = the referral ServiceRequest, and output = feedback. Its state model is harmonised with IHE 360X. I could not read the exact transition table, which is a diagram. https://build.fhir.org/ig/HL7/bser/workflow_management_resources.html
14. **[S] IHE 360X (Closed Loop Referral).** An IHE PCC supplement for referral request, then accept or decline, then progress, then outcome (consult note). ONC's project page lists it as Final Text in Aug 2024. Scheduling is optional. A continuous-build FHIR mapping maps Referral Request to ServiceRequest and Referral Decline to Task. I did not read the state list myself. https://www.ihe.net/uploadedFiles/Documents/PCC/IHE_PCC_Suppl_360X.pdf · https://build.fhir.org/ig/Virtually-Healthcare/R4/360X.html · https://www.healthit.gov/sites/default/files/facas/2023-03-09_360X_Presentation_508.pdf
15. **[S] SDOH Clinical Care IG pattern.** The performer's Task points to the referral source Task through `Task.partOf`. The performer's own ServiceRequest uses `intent = filler-order`, with `basedOn` pointing to the original. This is a worked example of option I. https://hl7.org/fhir/us/sdoh-clinicalcare/2022jan/exchange_workflow.html
16. **[V] Schedule and Slot are for bookable appointments.** Schedule is a container of slots that can be booked for appointments. `planningHorizon` is the period on offer, explicitly not a recurring template. Its actor is one schedulable resource. Rostering, shifts and on-call are not mentioned at all. https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/schedule.html
17. **[V] PractitionerRole availability.** R5 gathers R4's `availableTime`, `notAvailable` and `availabilityExceptions` into one `availability` element (the Availability datatype), alongside `period`, `active`, `specialty`, `location` and `healthcareService`. If availability differs by location or service, use a separate PractitionerRole for each. Nothing covers on-call or shifts. https://hl7.org/fhir/R5/practitionerrole.html
18. **[S/U] FHIR has no native duty-roster or on-call resource.** Searches of the spec and of PractitionerRole profiles found no on-call element or rota profile. Recurring opening hours (`availableTime`) and bookable Slots are the nearest fit, and neither models "who holds the cardiology on-call bleep tonight". This is absence of evidence, not a statement from HL7. https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/practitionerrole.html
19. **[V] IHE mCSD (v4.0.0, FHIR R4)** is a directory profile over Organization, Location, Practitioner, PractitionerRole, HealthcareService, Endpoint and OrganizationAffiliation, for facility and health-worker registries. It covers opening hours for facilities and services but not practitioner availability, on-call or shifts. https://profiles.ihe.net/ITI/mCSD/volume-1.html

## 2. How Epic, Oracle Cerner and the NHS handle inpatient consults

20. **[V] UCLA, Epic (Applied Clinical Informatics 2022, Zarrabi et al.).** Before the change, the consult order was optional and the consultant was reached by an operator page or a free-text page. The redesigned order sends a page to a division "virtual pager" that routes to whoever is on call now, with discrete fields for callback number and reason. Median time to the consultant opening the chart fell from 4.03 h to 0.63 h (p = 0.001), and the share opened within 1 h rose from 17% to 66%. Time to note filed did not change significantly. There was no in-basket message or consult list. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9259201
21. **[V] Salem Health, Epic rollout.** The initial consult note written in the Consult tab completes the consult order. For urgent or STAT consults, a direct call to the provider comes first and the order follows. Specialties not yet live keep calling the on-call provider. https://salemhealth.org/common-ground-home/common-ground-post/new-inpatient-consult-workflow-starts-april-30-in-epic
22. **[S] Epic consult order life cycle (local sites).** Search summaries say the order counts as complete once linked to a signed note, there is a consult patient list for the receiving service, one site targets completion within 24 h, and active consult orders are auto-cancelled 2 h after discharge. These are site-specific builds. https://www.salemhealth.org/common-ground-home/common-ground-post/changes-to-inpatient-consult-orders-in-epic
23. **[U] Epic "consult acknowledgement" as a formal step.** I found no public Epic documentation of an "acknowledge consult" status. The public sources show order, then a list or page, then a note linked to the order, then complete.
24. **[S] Alberta Connect Care (Epic).** When the note is written, the editor asks the author to pick from open consult orders, which links the note and updates the team's consult list. If the order is not completed, the patient stays on the list, and there is a separate "complete order" action. https://manual.connect-care.ca/workflows/ReferralConsultation/consultation-workflows
25. **[S] Oracle Cerner (PowerChart) local workflows.** Sites run "place consult order" in PowerOrders, then "consult note" in Dynamic Documentation, then a separate "Complete Consult order" step. Linking and auto-completion depend on each site's build. https://niagarahealth.on.ca/files/his_tip_sheets/PhysTrack-AcuteCare-PrescriberConsults2024-10-111141Approved-94.pdf · https://hi.northernlighthealth.org/Flyers/Quick-Reference-Guides-(QRGs)/Workflows/QRG-8430-Cardiologist-Acute.aspx
26. **[S] Epic Treatment Team and First Contact.** Treatment Team lists the clinicians on an inpatient stay, and Epic's On-Call Finder relies on the primary team being set. The newer "First Contact" label defaults to the attending and moves to whoever signs into that team. Epic's public table HSP_TRTMT_TEAM records a start time for each member. https://epicsupport.sites.uiowa.edu/node/331 · https://open.epic.com/EHITables/GetTable/HSP_TRTMT_TEAM.htm · https://salemhealth.org/common-ground-home/common-ground-post/epic-first-contact-available-nov.-20
27. **[S] First-Contact Provider study (University of Chicago, Jt Comm J Qual Patient Saf 2018; 44:107–113).** Requiring one named responsible practitioner per inpatient raised the share of critical results reported directly to that practitioner from 18.0% to 87.8%. 98.6% of patients had a first-contact provider documented at noon. https://psnet.ahrq.gov/web-mm/getting-right-doctor-right-away · https://aminer.org/pub/5c0f7711da562944ac70d627/who-s-covering-this-patient-developing-a-first-contact-provider-fcp-designation
28. **[S] Pages to the wrong doctor (Wong et al., Arch Intern Med 2009).** 14% of 10,190 pages at two Canadian teaching hospitals went to the wrong physician, mostly residents who were off duty. About 15% of those were emergency pages and 32% urgent. Fragmented links between call schedules, paging and care-team lists are the cause most often named. https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/10.1001/archinternmed.2009.117 · https://psnet.ahrq.gov/issue/frequency-and-clinical-importance-pages-sent-wrong-physician
29. **[S] Team-based pagers (Shieh et al., Jt Comm J 2014; 40:77–82).** Paging a team or role pager rather than an individual reduced paging errors in a large academic hospital. https://psnet.ahrq.gov:443/issue/assigning-team-based-pager-call-physicians-reduces-paging-errors-large-academic-hospital
30. **[V] RCEM "Referral for Inpatient Care Standards" (2025).** Hospitals must keep an up-to-date *digital on-call rota* for every specialty that takes referrals. While the specialty has not yet responded, the patient remains the ED's responsibility. Specialties should have experienced staff able to assess within **30 minutes**. ED IT must record the *date and time of referral*. The referrer must say whether the referral is for *advice or admission* and how urgent it is. Names are preferred to roles. Advice-only replies must be documented. Note: these cover ED-to-specialty referrals, not ward-to-ward. https://rcem.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Referral-for-Inpatient-Care-Standards.pdf
31. **[S] RCP and RCEM "better medical pathways" (2022).** Recommends same-day, or occasionally next-day, cross-specialty inpatient assessments when needed. https://rcem.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Rebuilding_the_NHS_better_medical_pathways_for_acute_care_2022_FINAL.pdf
32. **[V] RCP "Referring wisely" (2017)** concerns *which* inpatient referrals between physicians need a specialist. It gives no response-time standard. https://www.rcp.ac.uk/media/1ondl1nr/referring-wisely.pdf
33. **[U] RCP "Acute care toolkit" response time for ward specialty referrals.** I found no numeric standard for routine ward-to-specialty referrals (such as "within 24 h") in RCP or Academy of Medical Royal Colleges material. The 24 h figures I saw were local targets (Salem, Exeter). https://rcp.ac.uk/improving-care/resources/acute-care-toolkit-2-high-quality-acute-care
34. **[S] NHS Seven Day Services clinical standards (v2, 2022).** Standard 2 requires every emergency admission to have a consultant assessment within 14 h. Standard 8 (via NICE QS174) requires consultant review at least every 24 h after that, more often if clinically needed. These are review clocks a rota and responsibility model must support. https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/B1230-seven-day-services-clinical-standards-08-feb-2022.pdf · https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/qs174/chapter/quality-statement-3-consultant-assessment-and-review
35. **[S] Royal Devon & Exeter e-referrals (BMJ Open Quality 2018, Shephard et al.).** Replaced paper "white card" ward referrals (more than 130 a week), where the referring team sometimes dropped the patient before the receiving team had accepted. The new system added a specialty *acknowledge* step to track progress. Time from decision to referral improved from 2.1 h to 1.9 h, and patients were reviewed faster. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjoq-2017-000249
36. **[V] UCLH neurology, phone versus e-referral (Ziff et al., Postgrad Med J letter 2019).** On the phone line, 63% of incoming calls were missed and 44% of outgoing calls went unanswered. With email referrals, 0% were missed and all were acknowledged within 24 h, with an auto-reply that confirmed receipt. Recommends a hybrid: electronic referral plus phone for hyper-acute cases. https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10071362/3/Ziff_Modernising%20inpatient%20referral%20systems.%20Switching%20from%20On-Call%20to%20On-Line_AAM.pdf
37. **[V] Southland NZ e-referral (BMJ Open Quality 2022).** Mandatory fields included the registrar told, a one-line question, vitals with EWS, resuscitation status and the requesting consultant. The on-call registrar accepts or declines, a decline needs a reason, and the referrer is emailed. Review time was captured only when the reviewer clicked "acknowledge", and a third of staff never remembered, so 31–45% of delay data was missing. **Lesson: do not make a manual click the only timestamp.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9204417
38. **[V] Jerusalem 1,000-bed hospital (Isr J Health Policy Res 2021).** Mean time from request to consult documentation was 3.9 h for medical and 10.0 h for surgical consultants. Follow-up was documented in only 37% and 22%. Only 35–61% of requests were rated good. The authors recommend structured requests stating question and urgency, plus computerised notification. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8053423/
39. **[S] Stanford "Waiting it out" (Rahman et al., Postgrad Med J 2019; 95:1–5).** Across 316 internal medicine patients there were 53 consult or procedure delays, averaging 1.8 days each and 83 bed-days in total. Top causes were scheduling, late response to pages and a busy service. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30674619/
40. **[S] Careflow Connect (System C), NHS.** Northampton replaced faxed internal referrals with app-based referrals that are visible and audited, with the urgent ones phoned first and then recorded. Before this the trust had no metrics on referral volume or time to action. BHR reported ward referrals that had taken up to 2 days got faster. https://www.systemc.com/customer-stories/northampton-general-hospital-adopts-careflow-connect/ · https://www.digitalhealth.net/2021/06/barking-havering-and-redbridge-university-switch-to-electronic-referral
41. **[U] Nervecentre and Medway referral modules.** The only Nervecentre public material I found covers ED "speciality review" tasks on an urgent-care dashboard. I found no public documentation of an inpatient referral acceptance model. https://www.sfh-tr.nhs.uk/media/xvdj1wgl/uec-nervecentre-speciality-review-within-ed.pdf

## 3. Duty rotas, on-call and "who is responsible now"

42. **[S] Amion.** Provides a read-only "who's on call" view by service and day, with page or secure text from the list. It is often shown inside Epic through On-Call Finder, and some sites have since replaced Amion's "Who's On" page. https://med.uth.edu/msit/amion/ · https://ynhhs.org/publications/bulletin/011923/onecall
43. **[S] QGenda and Epic On-Call Finder.** QGenda stays the source of truth, and Epic On-Call Finder displays its schedules and lets users Secure Chat the on-call person or team. Integrations are one-way syncs, by REST API or CSV pushed to SFTP every few minutes, and QGenda is the fallback during Epic downtime. https://it.houstonmethodist.org/qgenda/ · https://texaschildrensnews.org/epic-on-call-finder-with-qgenda-integration-goes-live-july-13/
44. **[S] Spok.** Offers role-based messaging (by role, location or on-call status), and its operator console routes calls from live on-call schedules. Another vendor's site (Munson, Telmediq) says orders must not be sent by paging, so paging is a notification channel, not an order channel. https://resources.spok.com/physician-scheduling-software · https://www.munsonhealthcare.org/about-the-system/news-media-relations/news/telmediq-key-points-clinical-paging-process
45. **[S] NHS e-rostering (Allocate/RLDatix HealthMedics, Patchwork).** Doctors see their own and colleagues' on-call rotas, record call-outs, and book annual, study and sick leave. The systems handle swaps and leave requests with notified outcomes and check safe-working rules automatically. https://buildingbetterhealthcare.com/medway-nhs-foundation-trust-extends-e-rostering-rollout-to-550-medics-157460 · https://www.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud/services/269653056343097
46. **[S] BMA rostering rules for swaps.** A swap must be between doctors of commensurate grade, and the roster manager must check it does not breach hours or rest limits. https://www.bma.org.uk/pay-and-contracts/working-hours/work-schedule/managing-rotas-and-duty-rosters-for-junior-doctors-in-england/key-principles-for-managing-rosters · https://www.bma.org.uk/media/1979/bma-nhse-good-rostering-guidance-may2018.pdf
47. **[S] 2016 resident-doctor contract limits.** Maximum 72 h in any 168 h, a 48 h average, 13 h maximum shift, on-call periods up to 24 h, 11 h rest after nights. Non-resident on-call needs at least 5 h continuous rest between 22:00 and 07:00 and at least 8 h rest per 24 h. These are UK rules, not Ugandan ones. https://www.bma.org.uk/pay-and-contracts/working-hours/work-schedule/bma-rota-checker · https://nhsemployers.org/articles/information-guardians-safe-working-hours
48. **[S] BMA "Safe handover: safe patients" (2004).** Handover moves responsibility at shift change. It should be led by the most senior clinician present, and unclear roles are the recurring failure. https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1362271/safe-handover-safe-patients
49. **[S] Hospital at Night.** A multi-professional night team (senior or advanced nurse practitioners plus doctors). A clinical coordinator filters bleeps in set hours (for example 19:00–07:00), assigns each task to the most suitable member, and has it signed off when done. Several sites moved from bleeps to an electronic out-of-hours task list. https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/paediatric-guidelines-in-development/induction-and-handbooks/quick-guide-rhcyp-hospital-night-han-team · https://westerntrust.hscni.net/wp-content/uploads/wpfd/preview_files/Hospital_at_Night_Bleep_Policy(83e8ef518174e1eb6be4a0778d050c9d).pdf
50. **[V] Data-standards gap.** FHIR Schedule and Slot cover appointments, PractitionerRole covers recurring availability, and mCSD covers directories. None models a duty assignment ("person X covers role Y for scope Z from t1 to t2"). See findings 16–19.

## 4. Uganda, Mulago and low-resource settings

51. **[V] Mulago medicine is organised by sub-specialty, not general firms.** In 2007 the Directorate of Medicine set up sub-specialist units run by consultants, physicians and senior house officers. Patients go through Accident & Emergency and are then admitted to the right specialised unit. The hospital saw about 15,000 emergency medical visits a year and 900–1,200 medical-ward admissions a month (2011–14 data). Kalyesubula et al., PLoS One 2019. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6516645
52. **[S] Medical inpatient services moved from Mulago to Kiruddu.** This happened in 2014 during Mulago's renovation. A Mulago/Kiruddu cohort (2011–19, 108,357 adult medical admissions) found 16.7% inpatient mortality, with HIV in 24% of admissions, diabetes 12.7%, hypertension 12.2% and TB 8.8%. Kazibwe et al., Trop Med Health 2022. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9458845/
53. **[S] Kiruddu's mandate.** Kiruddu National Referral Hospital (200 beds) is described as mainly specialised internal medicine plus burns and reconstructive surgery. Kawempe handles maternity and paediatrics. A Nile Post report (undated in the result) mentions a proposed "downgrade" of Kiruddu. Its current status is unverified. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-36877-9.pdf · https://nilepost.co.ug/news/13701/kawempe-naguru-kiruddu-approved-as-referral-hospitals
54. **[V] Kiruddu history.** Opened 16 May 2016 as a general hospital (African Development Bank funded). It was part of a 2013 plan to move first-level load off Mulago so that Mulago takes referred patients only. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiruddu_General_Hospital
55. **[S] Mulago's current name and complex.** Now Mulago National Specialised Hospital (about 1,000 beds) within the Mulago complex, alongside Mulago Women's Referral Hospital, UHI, UCI and IDI. I could not confirm the date of the renaming. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulago_National_Specialised_Hospital
56. **[S] Ward team make-up.** A Mulago medical team is a consultant physician (the attending), senior house officers (MMed residents, called "SHOs"), interns and medical students. One account describes daily rounds with a hospitalist attending, an SHO, an intern and students. The MMed in Internal Medicine is a 3-year programme taught through Makerere and Mulago, and Kiruddu is also a Makerere teaching hospital. https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000007149 · https://som.mak.ac.ug/academic-programs/graduate-programs · https://chs.mak.ac.ug/muyu
57. **[U] Rotating "take" or admitting days by unit at Mulago or Kiruddu.** Not found in any public source. One visitor guide says every day is an admission day on the wards. Given finding 51, admission is probably by sub-specialty unit from A&E rather than a firm take rota. Confirm locally. https://www.med.umn.edu/sites/med.umn.edu/files/gmer-mulago-makerere-kampala-uganda-guide.pdf
58. **[S] Internship and UMDPC.** A one-year internship at a Council-approved hospital, split between a medical discipline (internal medicine or paediatrics) and a surgical one (surgery or obstetrics and gynaecology), under specialist supervision. A consultant must sign off each discipline on the internship form before the intern gets full registration. Interns often work about 10 h a day, 7 days a week. In 2025–26 the government introduced, and then ordered a review of, a new internship policy with a pre-internship exam. I did not read the UMDPC's own text. https://old.ulii.org/node/26551 · https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/special-reports/-why-medical-interns-are-usually-on-strike-1696178 · https://www.monitor.co.ug/uganda/news/national/museveni-orders-review-of-new-medical-internship-policy-5628002
59. **[S] Nursing grades at Mulago.** Health Service Commission adverts list Senior Nursing Officer (U3) and Nursing Officer (U4, for example ICU and A&E). Ward managers are typically Senior Nursing Officers (the "nurse in charge" or ward sister). A new Ministry of Public Service structure was approved for 2023/24, but its rollout is reported as delayed. https://hsc.go.ug/adverts/HSC%20Advert%20No.%204-December%202022.pdf · https://independent.co.ug/nursing-mulagos-staff-shortage
60. **[U] Uganda MoH National Referral Guidelines (2013).** Neither the document nor anything on health.go.ug turned up in search. Secondary sources describe Uganda's referral system as a referral note issued from a lower to a higher level of care, and the Uganda Clinical Guidelines 2023 carry level-of-care referral markers. The MoH Service Standards (2016) apply to all facilities. Check library.health.go.ug by hand. https://udn.or.ug/reviving-the-functionality-of-the-referral-system-in-uganda/
61. **[S] Mulago A&E medical emergencies (Okia thesis, 2016).** Of 305 patients, the median wait to see a doctor was 68 min, and the most acutely ill (red triage) waited longest at 91.5 min. 48 h mortality was 8.9%. https://dspace.mak.ac.ug/items/8df7e3d0-37e4-4b7c-a447-7d0fe5166d59
62. **[U] Studies of consult delays between departments in Mulago or East Africa.** None found. The nearest are in-hospital surgical delay at Soroti (22.7% of patients delayed, longer stays), a Kenyan qualitative study of the "third delay" for critically ill patients (bureaucratic processes), and an Ethiopian ED-to-ICU consult study run over Telegram. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38176878/ · https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00268-022-06591-0 · https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10088255/
63. **[S] Rota adherence is a real issue in Uganda.** Absenteeism is far lower in high-level public hospitals (no health worker present on under 5% of monitored days) than in low-level clinics (42%). Biometric clock-in at high-volume sites cut absenteeism from 69% to 41% (2015–18). A rota in the EHR should record *planned* cover, not assume it was delivered. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8378719/ · https://intrahealth.org/sites/default/files/attachment-files/innovation-tech-briefreducing-absenteeism.pdf

## Design implications

1. **Keep the request and its fulfilment separate.** Model the consult as a ServiceRequest-like row (status: draft, active, on-hold, revoked, completed, entered-in-error; priority: routine, urgent, asap, stat; with `reason_condition_id`) and its fulfilment as a Task-like row (requested, received, accepted, rejected, in-progress, completed, cancelled, failed). That puts acknowledgement and decline where FHIR, BSeR and 360X put them (findings 4, 6, 13, 14).
2. **Address the consult to a role or team, not a person.** Store both "requested performer type" (for example "Nephrology, on-call registrar") and the resolved owner. Work out the owner from the rota *at send time* and again *on hand-off*. Team or role addressing cuts misdirected pages (findings 3, 28, 29).
3. **Make the closing link structural.** The consult note must reference the request (event `basedOn` request), and signing it should move the Task to completed. The Epic and Cerner pattern is that the note completes the order, and where it does not, open orders linger on lists (findings 5, 21, 24, 25).
4. **Timestamps come from actions, never from a separate "acknowledge" click.** Opening the request, accepting it, the first note and completion should each stamp time automatically. Southland lost 31–45% of its data to a manual acknowledge button (findings 20, 37).
5. **A decline needs a reason, and declining should not quietly bounce the patient back.** RCEM forbids handing a referred patient back. Southland and 360X require a decline reason. Responsibility stays with the referring team until the receiving team accepts or sees the patient, and the UI should say so (findings 30, 37, 14).
6. **Every consult states advice or review or take-over, its urgency, and a one-line question.** RCEM, Jerusalem and Southland all point here. Auto-fill vitals, NEWS2 and resuscitation status rather than retyping them (findings 30, 37, 38).
7. **Response clocks should be configurable per hospital, with no hard-coded national number.** The only firm UK figure is RCEM's 30 min for ED referrals. Ward-to-ward figures are local (often 24 h, or same day for urgent). Uganda has no published standard. Show elapsed time against each hospital's target and escalate to the next rung of the rota (findings 30, 33, 22).
8. **Plan for a hybrid channel.** Urgent and STAT consults are phoned first and recorded after. The system must allow "already discussed with X at hh:mm" without that blocking the record (findings 21, 36, 40).
9. **The rota needs its own data model, because FHIR has none.** Store duty assignments as (person, role or position, scope: unit, ward or specialty, start, end, kind: rostered, on-call, cover or swap), with an escalation chain (intern, then SHO/resident, then consultant). Keep the planned rota distinct from confirmed presence (sign-on), since rota adherence is not assured (findings 16–19, 50, 63).
10. **Keep "who is responsible for this bed now" as its own explicit record**, close to Epic's Treatment Team and First Contact and to FHIR's Encounter.participant ATND plus a CareTeam with participant periods. Have handover write to it, with one named first-contact clinician per patient at all times (findings 11, 12, 26, 27, 48).
11. **Model the Mulago and Kiruddu unit as a sub-specialty unit** (consultants, SHOs, interns, nurse in charge) admitting from A&E. Treat any "take day" rotation as optional configuration until confirmed on site. An intern's actions should carry their supervising consultant for UMDPC sign-off (findings 51, 56–58).
12. **Swaps and leave are rota edits with an audit trail and a grade-equivalence check.** Mirror the BMA rule that a swap must be between doctors of commensurate grade, without importing UK hour limits as hard rules for Uganda (findings 45–47).

**Not verified, and worth checking:** the text of Uganda's MoH referral guidelines, the UMDPC internship regulations, whether Mulago or Kiruddu medicine runs unit take days, Kiruddu's current designation, an Epic consult acknowledgement status, the Nervecentre and Medway inpatient referral models, and any RCP numeric target for ward referrals.
